Two design points I want on the record because they are easy to get wrong and
this candidate got them right:

1. **Stating correspondence on `targetIterate` rather than "reaches the exit"
   is the correct choice, and for the stated reason.** The blocks have no `halt`
   at their exit, so a fuel-budget claim could overshoot and execute past the
   exit — "reaches the block exit" would then not be a claim about the block.
2. **The mutation design defeats a real vacuity trap.** Cases A–E edit the block
   *and its fetch lemma together*, because Lean admits a failed declaration at
   its stated type: a block-only edit fails at the fetch inventory while every
   downstream operational theorem still elaborates, so it would test the
   inventory rather than the correspondence. Cases C and E further leave the
   immediate inventories `[2,2,2,2]` / `[2,2,2,0,2,2]` intact, demonstrating
   that the syntactic checks R2 relied on could not have caught them. That is
   the P2-1 lesson turned into a discriminating test.

cell because there was nowhere else to put it — cannot recur. This is a better
fix than restoring R1's delimiters alone.
